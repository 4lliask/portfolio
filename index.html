Starcraft II - Sound Re Design

I redesigned all the sound for this part of the original cut, and composed the music that plays throughout.

The funniest sound to make was the Vikings (the aircraft at the beginning), their engines were fully synthesized in Serum 2. The Viking that stops to shoot the Ultralisk (the beast) was another highlight, it was built by layering recordings of gears, metal hits, can foley, and impacts from EDM libraries, its machine guns were completely synthesized in Serum 2. For the Ultralisks, I used growls from different animals, stretched to sound heavier and give them that "alien" feel. The tanks combine gear sounds, cinematic impacts, foley of cans and metal hits, I used the same elements for the shot showing the footstep and their fire, along with real gunshot recordings. The Zerglings (the little creatures following the Ultralisk) keep the game's original sounds. For the creature emerging from the ground, I combined earthquake SFX from EDM libraries, debris foley, and cinematic impacts. Its roar is a dubstep growl stretched heavily to sound less robotic and more organic.

The music was fun to make too. My original plan was to leave the redesign without a score, but once I got into final details I realized it needed one. I wanted a tribal feel, since the Zerg (the aliens) are a fully biological faction in the series. That's part of why I added a didgeridoo to the track.

Overwatch - Sound Re Design

I redesigned all the sound for this part of the original cut.

I must say that at first it was hard to nail a good sound for the woodpecker, I instinctively tried with recordings of wood being hit, which I discarded later, the thing that clicked was a percussion sound from a music library, I layered this with a snare that had a similar timbre, and then added a gunshot sample. I later put to this group of sounds a disperser, which is a plugin that stacks all-pass filters to smear and stretch transients. Next in the woodpecker chain we got a Faturator by Kilohearts, which is a distortion plugin, this one was automated to be active only at the last woodpecker appearance to make it harder and give the viewer the feeling of what Bastion (the robot) was hearing. I used Pigments synth to make the beeps emitted by Bastion, layers of stomp effects and pieces of a stock robot walking SFX for his movement. For his "transformation" I used a combination of gear sounds, foley of cans, metal pieces, and special effects from music libraries. The machine gun was the sound I enjoyed making the most, it was again fully synthesized in Serum 2 and has two layers, one focused on the high end and one on the low end, each of these two layers has a disperser to make the transients of the machine gun heavier. The crackling of the trees being shot comes from layered recordings of falling trees and wood pieces being shot. For the music I added a simple drone from a music library, and for ambiances I used stock forest recordings.

About me

I have had a relationship with sound and its different branches since I was a teenager. I started out making tracks independently and just for fun, which led me to study Sound Engineering, graduating from DUOC UC.

I have accumulated professional experience since my first year of college, starting as a freelance sound designer and composer. After this I got a junior job at a local TV channel, where I performed well enough to become the sound director for almost all of the channel's programs, and to be in charge of sound for the channel's coverage of the Santiago 2023 Pan American Games. I stayed in this role for almost two years, while also taking on live show jobs in the local scene. In 2025 I stopped working at the channel. I still took on freelance jobs in different areas, and later that year I got the Field Acoustic Engineer role at a company dedicated to environmental acoustics and regulatory compliance.

Now I dedicate my free time to making music, sound design, and sound work in general. I usually work on EDM music, like Drum and Bass, Brostep, Neurofunk, or House, but I also like to branch into other genres, like Latin music, experimental rap beats, or themed music like the ones shown in the projects section. I enjoy every part of the career I chose, and video games are one of the spaces I'm most excited to start contributing to, a medium I've been part of as a player since I was a little kid.
